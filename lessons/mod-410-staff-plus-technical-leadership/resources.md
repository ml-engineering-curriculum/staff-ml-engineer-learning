# Resources for mod-410-staff-plus-technical-leadership

Real, citable references for the topics this module covers. Read the canonical staff-plus and management texts before the module; treat the operating-model references (Amazon PR/FAQ, OKR, Wardley) as vocabulary to use in your artifacts rather than ceremony to import wholesale; treat the hiring-process references as the evidence base behind chapter 4's rubric discipline.

## Canonical staff-plus IC references

- **Larson, Will.** *Staff Engineer: Leadership beyond the management track.* Stripe Press, 2021. [staffeng.com/book](https://staffeng.com/book). The load-bearing reference for the four archetypes (Tech Lead / Architect / Solver / Right Hand), the roadmap-as-strategy framing chapter 2 leans on, the *"working on what matters"* chapter that grounds chapter 2's resource-ask defence, and the hiring-and-writing chapters that ground chapter 4. The archetype vocabulary is the same across mod-401, this module's chapters 1 and 7, and the sustaining-bar discipline.
- **Larson, Will.** *An Elegant Puzzle: Systems of Engineering Management.* Stripe Press, 2019. Grounds chapter 4's rubric-based-loop mechanics (org-scale hiring-process design) and chapter 5's coaching-cadence patterns. The chapter on *"How to run great meetings"* is directly applicable to chapter 3's exec-audience meeting rhythms.
- **Reilly, Tanya.** *The Staff Engineer's Path.* O'Reilly, 2022. Chapters on big-picture thinking, on writing for a broader audience, and on influence-without-authority ground chapters 1, 3, and 5. Reilly's naming of the *review / mentor / sponsor* trio underlies chapter 5's three coaching modes.
- **Skelton, Matthew and Pais, Manuel.** *Team Topologies: Organizing Business and Technology Teams for Fast Flow.* IT Revolution, 2019. [teamtopologies.com/book](https://teamtopologies.com/book). The stream-aligned / platform-team / enabling-team split is the substrate under mod-401 chapter 5's staff/EM partnership contract and under chapter 5's coaching-boundary discussion in this module.
- **Winters, Titus; Manshreck, Tom; Wright, Hyrum.** *Software Engineering at Google.* O'Reilly, 2020. Free HTML edition at [abseil.io/resources/swe-book](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book). The chapters on *"Leading a Team"*, *"Documentation"*, and *"Large-Scale Changes"* ground the staff-plus operating patterns chapters 2 and 5 rely on.

## Strategy, decision-making, and roadmap texts

- **Rumelt, Richard.** *Good Strategy Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters.* Crown Business, 2011. Chapter 3 ("The Kernel of Good Strategy" — diagnosis / guiding policy / coherent action) is the load-bearing reference for chapter 2's diagnosis section and for the non-goals-as-first-class rule. Chapter 4 ("Bad Strategy") is the specific reference for chapter 2's *feature list* and *wish list from leadership* failure modes.
- **Doerr, John.** *Measure What Matters.* Portfolio, 2018. [whatmatters.com/the-book/](https://www.whatmatters.com/the-book/). The OKR discipline chapter 2's resource-ask-tied-to-delta pattern maps onto. Not a required reference — the delta-per-program pattern stands on its own — but useful for a learner who has to translate the roadmap into OKR vocabulary for the exec cycle.
- **Cagan, Marty.** *Inspired: How to Create Tech Products Customers Love.* Second edition, Wiley, 2017. The product-vs-engineering split of the roadmap conversation chapter 2 sits inside; useful for staff engineers whose portfolio is heavily product-partnered.

## Executive-audience writing and working-backwards

- **Bryar, Colin and Carr, Bill.** *Working Backwards: Insights, Stories, and Secrets from Inside Amazon.* St. Martin's Press, 2021. The canonical reference for Amazon's PR/FAQ working-backwards discipline (chapter 4 of the book). Chapter 3 of this module leans on it for the new-program framing genre.
- **Bezos, Jeff.** *1997 Amazon Shareholder Letter.* [PDF via Amazon investor relations](https://s2.q4cdn.com/299287126/files/doc_financials/annual/Shareholderletter97.pdf). The canonical source of the Type 1 / Type 2 reversibility axis chapter 3 uses in exec-doc trade-off framing and chapter 6 uses for vendor decisions.
- **Bezos, Jeff.** *2015 Amazon Shareholder Letter.* [PDF via Amazon investor relations](https://s2.q4cdn.com/299287126/files/doc_financials/annual/2015-Letter-to-Shareholders.PDF). The specific letter where the Type 1 / Type 2 framing is stated in the form chapter 3 quotes; useful for the primary-source citation in an exec doc.
- **Minto, Barbara.** *The Pyramid Principle: Logic in Writing and Thinking.* Third edition, Pearson, 2009. The top-down / *"answer first, then support it"* structure chapter 3's one-pager is built on.
- **Amazon shareholder letter archive.** [Amazon Investor Relations — Annual Reports & Proxies](https://ir.aboutamazon.com/annual-reports-proxies-and-shareholder-letters/default.aspx). The wider archive contains many worked examples of the compressed exec-audience narrative style chapter 3 draws on.
- **Amazon Leadership Principles.** [amazon.jobs/en/principles](https://www.amazon.jobs/en/principles). Referenced in *Working Backwards*; the *"Are Right, A Lot"* and *"Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit"* principles are the operating-culture counterparts to chapter 3's *hedged recommendation* failure mode and chapter 2's *no in writing* discipline.

## Management and staff/EM partnership references

- **Fournier, Camille.** *The Manager's Path: A Guide for Tech Leaders Navigating Growth and Change.* O'Reilly, 2017. The EM ladder from tech lead through VP; chapter 1 of this module uses it to name the boundary the staff engineer holds against crossing into the EM path. Chapter 4 of this module cites Fournier for the *tie goes to no-hire* rule (via her blog posts on senior hiring). Chapter 5's *"if coaching does not work"* section reads against her chapter on managing underperforming engineers.
- **Fournier, Camille.** [*Elided Branches* blog](https://skamille.medium.com/) and posts on the older [elidedbranches.blogspot.com](https://elidedbranches.blogspot.com/). Additional posts on staff-plus hiring, engineering-manager tenure, and senior-engineer calibration. <!-- needs-research: pin down the specific Fournier post that names *tie goes to no-hire* once WebSearch is available; the phrase is Fournier-adjacent in her writing on interviewing. -->
- **Hogan, Lara.** *Resilient Management.* A Book Apart, 2019. The sponsor / mentor / coach distinction chapter 5's three coaching modes borrow from. The chapter on providing feedback grounds chapter 5's *"technical-bar read is the staff engineer's"* discipline.
- **Zhuo, Julie.** *The Making of a Manager: What to Do When Everyone Looks to You.* Portfolio, 2019. Referenced in chapter 1 as the outside-in read on where the EM job actually is; useful for the staff engineer holding the boundary against absorbing EM work.
- **Majors, Charity.** [*The Engineer/Manager Pendulum*](https://charity.wtf/2017/05/11/the-engineer-manager-pendulum/). Blog post, 2017. The framing chapter 7 cites for the sideways move between staff-plus IC and EM as a healthy career pattern.

## Hiring, structured interviewing, and calibration

- **Google re:Work.** [Hiring — Structured interviewing](https://rework.withgoogle.com/subjects/hiring-decisions/). Google's practitioner writeup on why structured interviews outperform unstructured ones; the direct evidence base under chapter 4's rubric discipline.
- **Schmidt, Frank L. and Hunter, John E.** *The Validity and Utility of Selection Methods in Personnel Psychology.* Psychological Bulletin 124(2), 1998. The canonical meta-analysis chapter 4 references for the structured-vs-unstructured-interviewing validity gap. [DOI: 10.1037/0033-2909.124.2.262](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.124.2.262).
- **Google re:Work.** [Guide: Hire](https://rework.withgoogle.com/guides/hiring-use-structured-interviewing/steps/introduction/). The step-by-step how-to that chapter 4's rubric authoring reads against. Includes worked examples of the rubric-dimension × level structure.
- **Larson, Will.** [*Hiring a staff engineer*](https://lethain.com/hiring-staff-engineer/) and [*Interviewing engineering managers*](https://lethain.com/hiring-engineering-manager/). Staff-plus practitioner posts on the loop-design and calibration mechanics chapter 4 grounds on.
- **StaffEng.com.** [Interviews and stories](https://staffeng.com/stories/). The stories archive is a useful calibration read for what staff-plus hiring conversations look like across companies.
